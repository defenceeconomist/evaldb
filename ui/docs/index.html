A working collection of concise evaluations and relevant audit reports to inform UK Ministry of Defence business cases.

The library helps analysts find evidence, judge its fit to a decision and trace the claim back to the original report. It supports professional judgement; being included does not mean a study proves that an intervention works in MOD.

What this handbook records

  • Selection criteria: what belongs in the collection and how exceptions are handled.
  • Content review: a decision, rationale and application boundary for every existing report.
  • Using evidence: finding and citing material for a business case.
  • Design decisions: the interface review, implemented recommendations and trade-offs.
  • Maintenance: how to curate, rebuild, review and update the collection.

Purpose and scope

The intended users are MOD business-case authors, analysts, delivery teams and reviewers. The main task is to find an applicable lesson or finding, understand its limitations, and use it to challenge assumptions about options, costs, benefits, risk or delivery.

The current collection contains public reports from the screened catalogue. It is a targeted collection, not an exhaustive or representative review. UK defence evidence is particularly relevant, but international defence and selected civilian comparators can inform a specific mechanism or decision. Nationality, publisher reputation and recency are not quality scores.

Editorial boundaries

Report findings and recommendations remain separate from analyst judgements about MOD relevance, business-case uses and transfer. Summaries retain source locators and limitations. Missing conclusions or causal estimates are not invented. Findings describe the report’s evidence period, not necessarily the current position.

The September 2026 review screens the existing catalogue against the new policy. It does not independently reanalyse underlying datasets, validate every numerical claim, or establish the effectiveness of every intervention. Source limitations and outstanding verification needs remain visible.

Guidance informing the design

The five business-case filters follow the strategic, economic, commercial, financial and management dimensions described in HM Treasury’s Green Book (2026). Evidence types and the separation of process, impact and value-for-money questions draw on the Magenta Book (updated May 2026). These links were consulted on 17 September 2026. The library’s selection rules are an editorial policy, not an official MOD or Treasury standard.
